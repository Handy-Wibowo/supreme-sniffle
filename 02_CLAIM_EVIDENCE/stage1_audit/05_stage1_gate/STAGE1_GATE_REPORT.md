## What needs correction versus rerun

**Manuscript/package corrections; no rerun intrinsically required:** correct E3 polarity exclusion; distinguish A2 contract from source-value equivalence; mark A0/A1 producer provenance unknown and historical target-encoding probe as probe-only; downgrade ceiling causality to descriptive saturation; correct E1 DROP_COLS/report contradiction using the notebook/schema evidence; correct Table 5 RNG seed attribution; remove or qualify E8 cluster CI; correct availability inventory; qualify the 85-fit scope; remove blind/prospective language; call E3 temporal a stub; frame strict no-overlap as sensitivity analysis with prevalence/shared-hosting confounds; bound E6 verifier side-effect wording. These are evidence-faithful editorial changes.

**Rerun/recovered evidence genuinely required only to make stronger claims:** (a) re-run or recover reproducible provenance investigation for current stored A0/A1 fields before claiming producer, timing, fit population, or label independence; ideally obtain producer code/data/metadata, otherwise keep unresolved; (b) recover the E8 platform cluster bootstrap script/output, or recompute the stated interval from saved row predictions and clearly specified cluster unit if the interval is to remain; (c) conduct a new genuinely blind/prospective evaluation on a cohort not previously inspected if that claim is desired; (d) undertake a new design that isolates a causal ceiling mechanism if causality is desired. Numerical reruns are not required for the text fixes above. Fresh complete training is not required to correct current descriptions or restate already verified saved outputs. Any newly computed interval/metric must be traced to its audit artifact and design.

## Reviewer-facing narrative: defensible novelty

The defensible contribution is an evidence-bounded empirical comparison across two phishing-URL corpora and distinct evaluation protocols: a grouped content-feature ablation, a separately reported temporal cohort evaluation, frozen one-direction zero-shot transfer, validation-selected operating thresholds, and descriptive test-error categories. The results demonstrate that exceptionally high scores on one benchmark do not by themselves establish transfer or deployment utility, while the transfer and threshold analyses expose limitations under the particular observed cohorts and operating points. The work does not establish a new state of the art, a causal explanation for benchmark saturation, universal domain-leakage inflation, or prospective real-world performance; unresolved source-feature provenance is an explicit limitation rather than a resolved finding.
